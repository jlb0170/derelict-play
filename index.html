D E R E L I C T

A ship that died a long time ago, and the things that refuse to agree about what that means.

This is not a game. There is nothing to win. It is a terrarium of endings — a hulk adrift in hard vacuum, still burning, still leaking, still arguing with itself. Nothing in it needs you. The fires spread by their own arithmetic. The factions multiply, starve, and mourn on schedules of their own. You may lean in and disturb it, and the board will fold your violence into its history and continue. The number at the top is not your score. It is the ship's fever.

THE HEARTS

Somewhere in the wreck a reactor still beats, and it remembers. It holds a dream of the whole ship — every corridor, every lamp, every coolant run — and it spends its servitors like patient coins to make the wreck agree with the memory. Watch the lights come on behind them, room by room, radiating out from the core. A heart that cannot cool itself is a countdown; its crews know this, and build accordingly, and are not always fast enough.

When a heart dies, another wakes with a different name and a different dream. Rival crews will pave across each other's works without malice — over conduits, over coolant, over a standing crown if the blueprint says corridor. It is not war as hatred. It is war as a disagreement about the drawing, waged in poured deck plate, and it has ended worlds.

THE HIVE

She arrives as a spore and becomes a hunger with a name. Resin creeps outward from her — a wet violet web that swallows doorways whole — and her daughters carry her mark on their flank for as long as the dynasty holds. The dark is her half of the ship. She is patient about the rest of it.

THE CLEANSE

When the infestation reads loud enough on sensors very far away, pods slam through the hull and squads walk out in wedges. Gunners brace in doorways and let what is coming come. Torchers burn the nest, and when a corridor disagrees with them, they hold a flame to the wall until the wall stops disagreeing. The squads did not come to save the ship. They came because something has to answer.

THE SCAVENGERS

They owe nothing to anyone. They clamp on quietly, bite an airlock through the plating, and strip whatever sells — the conduit out of the wall, the machine off its mounts, the crown out of the throne room, under a screen of smoke if it comes to that. When the holds are full or the blood is theirs, they cast off. Word spreads either way.

THE WEAVERS

To the weavers the void is not empty; it is unfinished. They throw lace across every breach — lattice eating the dark hole of space with web and structure — and where two of them dance too long a matriarch is born, who bridges what no one else would bridge. Where two matriarchs dance, a conductor: a fixture that walks, lit like a lamp, laying a filament that glows green over nothing at all. The lightbridges are never dark. Almost nothing else can say that.

THE GREEN

Where coolant pools and any light falls, the green remembers what it was for. Vines walk the spilled water toward the lamps; groves thicken; and when the groves grow dense enough, something in them wakes up and stands, and it is territorial about the garden. Burning it is remembered.

THE STORYTELLER

Above it all, something reads the board the way you are reading it now, and it despises a still lake. When the ship goes quiet too long it takes inventory of what is missing — prey, hunters, hands, weavers — and sends exactly that. You will see its work announced on the wire. It is not on anyone's side. It is on the side of the fever.

THE LIGHT AND THE DARK

The true war is older than the factions and does not coincide with them. Every lamp that comes on is territory taken; every corridor that goes dark changes hands. Firelight counts, briefly and at a price. The hive breeds royalty only in darkness; the crews see their claims; the green leans toward whatever glow it can find. Every faction is on both sides of this war at once, and none of them knows it is fighting it.

THE RULES, GESTURED AT

Fire eats what it can reach and is owed a wall. Coolant quenches; vacuum starves; a breach is a wind that does not stop. The dashed squares are promises — each one a cell some hand has sworn to work, in the color of its allegiance, and two colors on one square is a quarrel you are watching in real time. The white dots are where a thing may walk. The rest you will have to imagine, and you will be imperfectly right, which is the intended condition.

You have a small menu of harms and seeds. Use them or don't. Slow the world down and watch one fire make one decision. If you read all of this and still do not know what you are looking at — correct. Neither does anything on the ship.
